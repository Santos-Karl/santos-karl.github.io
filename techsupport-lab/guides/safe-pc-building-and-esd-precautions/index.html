Safe PC building and ESD precautions

How to handle components safely during a build or repair: static protection, unplugging, and what never to open.

Overview

Most hardware damage during a build comes from static discharge, forcing connectors, or working on a live system. Work slowly, unplugged, and not on carpet.

Requirements

  • Anti-static wrist strap, or touch bare metal of the case before handling parts.
  • Phillips screwdriver, cable ties, and the motherboard manual.

Step-by-step instructions

  1. Unplug the PC, then press the power button once to drain residual power.
  2. Ground yourself by touching the unpainted metal of the case before handling each part.
  3. Hold components by their edges. Do not touch gold connectors or chips.
  4. Install the CPU by aligning the triangle marker, then install the cooler and RAM in the slots named in the manual.
  5. Connect the front panel header using the labels in the manual, not guesswork.
  6. Check every connector is fully seated before closing the case.

Troubleshooting notes

  • Use the RAM slot pair described in the motherboard manual. Two modules usually go in the paired slots, not next to each other.

Common mistakes

  • Forcing a CPU into the socket.
  • Plugging front panel cables into the wrong pins.
  • Building on carpet, which generates static.

Security and safety warnings

  • Never open or disassemble a power supply unit.
  • Do not work on a plugged-in PC.

Verification steps

  • The PC powers on and reaches the BIOS or UEFI screen.
  • BIOS shows the CPU, the RAM amount, and the storage drives.
